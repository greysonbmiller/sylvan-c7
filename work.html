How We Work

The work you can judge us on today.

Sylvan C7 is a new studio, and we're taking on our first client projects now. We could pad this page with work we didn't do or case studies nobody can check. We'd rather tell you that plainly and then be specific about the part that actually decides whether you end up with a good website: how the project is run, what lands in your hands at each stage, and the standards the code is held to.

A studio that's straight with you about what it hasn't done yet is a studio you can believe about scope, price, and timeline. That's the whole argument — and everything below is checkable before you spend a dollar.

The Process

How a project actually runs

Five steps, from the first conversation to a site you own outright. Each one ends with something concrete in your hands — not a status update. Ongoing care after launch is an optional add-on, not a sixth step.

01

Discovery call

A call, by phone or video, about your business rather than about websites: who your customers are, what you need the site to make happen, what you already have (domain, logo, photos, an existing host), and what's been frustrating about whatever you're using now. No pitch deck, no pressure to decide on the call.

You receive

A written summary of the call: the pages discussed, the features you need, the content you'd be responsible for supplying, and anything that could complicate the build. Yours to keep, whether or not you hire us.

02

Fixed quote in writing

We scope the project and put a price on it in writing before any work begins. It's a fixed price, not an hourly estimate that drifts: if the build takes longer than expected, that's our problem, not a surprise line on your invoice. The number moves only if you ask for something outside the agreed scope, and then only after you approve the change in writing.

You receive

A fixed-price quote itemized by what's included, with the timeline, the payment schedule, and an explicit list of what is not included, so nothing is ambiguous later. See pricing for how quotes are put together.

03

Design you approve

You see the design as a design first. We work up the key pages — usually the homepage and the templates the rest of the site inherits from — and revise them with you. How many rounds of revisions are included depends on the package you choose, and the exact number is written into your quote before you commit to anything. Changing a layout at this stage is a conversation; changing it after the site is built is a rebuild. Nothing gets coded until you've signed off on the design.

You receive

Design concepts for the key pages, in both desktop and mobile widths, revised through the rounds your package includes — plus a written note of exactly what you approved, so we're both building from the same thing.

04

Build and review

The approved design gets hand-coded. You get a private preview link early on and you're asked to look at it at fixed checkpoints: once the structure is in place, once your real content is loaded, and once it's polished. Open it on your own phone, your own laptop, the tablet at the front desk. Testing on the devices your customers actually use is the only testing that counts.

You receive

A private staging URL you can open on any device at any time, and your feedback at every checkpoint tracked in writing so nothing you asked for quietly gets lost — drawn from the revision rounds your package includes, as set out in your quote.

05

Launch, training and handover

We handle the launch itself: domain and DNS, hosting, SSL, analytics, search console, and redirects from the old site so existing links keep working. Then we hand over the keys. Every account is registered in your name with you as the owner — not ours with you as a guest — and we walk you through editing the live site yourself.

You receive

Owner access to the domain, hosting, and analytics accounts; the complete source code; a recorded walkthrough of the updates you'll make yourself; and a written handover document covering where everything lives and who to call.

After the five steps — optional add-on

Ongoing care, only if you want it

This is an add-on, not a sixth step. If you'd rather run the site yourself after handover, you'll be able to: it's yours, it's documented, and it lives in your own accounts. If you'd rather keep updates, backups, and monitoring off your plate, that's a month-to-month arrangement you can end whenever you like, and ending it stops the maintenance, not the website.

You receive

Optional ongoing maintenance — updates, backups, uptime monitoring, and a direct line for changes — with no contract that holds your site hostage if you stop. Details on the pricing page.

The Baseline

What you get, every time

These aren't upgrades or optional line items. Every site we build will include them, at every budget, because a site without them isn't finished. It's the standard we hold every build to.

Hand-written code

Your site is written in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript by a person, not exported from a page builder. That's the difference between a page that loads in a blink and one dragging along template machinery it never uses.

Responsive on every device

Designed for the phone first, because that's where most of your visitors are, then scaled up through tablet and desktop. Checked on real hardware, not just by dragging a browser window narrower.

Accessibility from the start

Semantic markup, keyboard navigation, real alt text, readable contrast, and properly labelled forms — built in as we go rather than patched on at the end, which is the only way it ever actually holds.

Fast load times

Images compressed and correctly sized, fonts and scripts kept lean, nothing loaded that the page doesn't need. Speed is measurable, so we measure it before launch instead of asserting it afterward.

SEO fundamentals

A unique title and meta description per page, a sensible heading structure, clean URLs, a sitemap, and your business details marked up so search engines and map listings read them correctly.

Analytics you can read

Analytics installed and configured in your own account, with the things that matter to you — calls, form submissions, direction requests — tracked as goals, so you can tell whether the site is earning its keep.

Full ownership

The domain, the hosting, the analytics, and the code are yours, in accounts registered to you. No proprietary platform, no licence to renew, nothing that stops working if you and we part ways.

Documentation and training

A written handover document and a recorded walkthrough of the edits you'll make yourself. You shouldn't have to pay somebody to change your own opening hours.

Verify, Don't Trust

Standards we hold ourselves to

Claims are cheap. These four are things you can check for yourself rather than take on faith — three of them with free tools, on this page right now.

Semantic, accessible HTML

Real headings, landmark elements, labelled form fields, and alt text on every meaningful image. Nothing that only works if you can see it and use a mouse.

  • Check it with WAVE, axe DevTools, or your browser's built-in accessibility inspector — on this page, right now.

Performance budgets

The budget is a published number, not a vague promise: under 500 KB of total page weight, and a Largest Contentful Paint — the moment the main content finishes appearing — under 2.5 seconds on a throttled 4G connection, not on fast office wifi. If a feature won't fit the budget, we find a lighter way to do it or tell you what it would cost in speed before you decide.

  • Check it with Google PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse, on any URL we hand you — those two numbers are exactly what it reports.

No lock-in, no proprietary platform

We build on open web standards and host on infrastructure you can buy directly. There's no Sylvan C7 platform to subscribe to, no builder account that has to stay active, and nothing that degrades if you walk away.

  • Check it by asking, before you sign, what breaks if you move hosts — then hold us to the answer, which is nothing.

Code another developer can pick up

Readable, commented, version-controlled source, structured the way a working developer expects to find it. The real test of ownership is whether you could hand the project to somebody else tomorrow without them wincing, so we write it to be handed off.

  • Check it at handover by having any developer you trust read the source — it's delivered readable and version-controlled, so a successor can audit every line.
