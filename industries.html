Industries

Every industry sells differently.
Every site should be built differently too.

A restaurant and a law firm are not competing for the same click, and they shouldn't get the same website. Below is how we think about eight different worlds — what each one's customers are actually trying to do, and what the site has to accomplish to win them.

Restaurants & Hospitality

A restaurant site's only jobs are to make people hungry and make it effortless to act on it. That means a menu that's never out of date and a reservation flow that doesn't lose a party of four on a Friday night.

  • Menu that owners can update themselves
  • Reservation or waitlist requests that land in one place
  • Hours, location, and specials visible in one glance
  • Photography that sells the food, not the template
Discuss a restaurant site

Dental & Medical Practices

Someone landing on a practice site is usually a little anxious and in a hurry. The site's job is to get them to a new-patient appointment request fast, and answer the insurance question before they have to call and ask.

  • New-patient appointment request front and center
  • Accepted insurance listed plainly, no guessing
  • Provider bios that build trust before the first visit
  • Mobile-first — most of this traffic is one thumb, one hand
Discuss a dental site

Legal & Professional Services

Nobody hires a firm off vibes alone. The site has to establish credibility fast, make practice areas easy to scan, and give a nervous prospective client a low-pressure way to reach out about their specific situation.

  • Practice areas organized the way clients think, not the way lawyers file
  • Attorney credentials and case-type focus, clearly stated
  • Confidential-feeling consultation request form
  • Fast load and clean typography — it reads as competence
Discuss a law firm site

Home Services & Trades

Most of these visitors are standing in a driveway or on a job site, comparing three companies on their phone. The site that wins is the one where getting a quote takes under a minute, not the one with the nicest logo.

  • Quote request form built for a thumb, not a mouse
  • Service area and service list stated up front
  • Click-to-call that actually works on mobile
  • Before/after project photos as proof of work
Discuss a home services site

Fitness & Wellness

A gym or studio site has one real conversion moment: getting a stranger to try the first class. Everything else — schedules, coach bios, membership tiers — exists to remove the reasons not to walk in.

  • Class schedule that's always current
  • Free trial or intro offer with a one-step signup
  • Membership tiers explained without jargon
  • Energy in the photography and motion, not stock gym shots
Discuss a fitness site

Retail & E-commerce

Retail sites live or die on how fast someone can find a product and how little friction stands between "interested" and "checked out." Every extra click or slow-loading page is a customer handed to a competitor.

  • Clear product categories and fast search
  • Checkout that doesn't demand an account to buy
  • Inventory and pricing that stay accurate
  • Fast page loads — retail visitors bounce quickest
Discuss an e-commerce site

SaaS & Technology

A SaaS site has seconds to explain what the product actually does before a skeptical visitor leaves. Clarity beats cleverness — the copy has to say what problem this solves, for whom, and what happens when they click the button.

  • A homepage that explains the product in one screen
  • Pricing that's transparent, not "contact sales" for everything
  • Free trial or demo request with minimal friction
  • Integrations and use cases scannable, not buried
Discuss a SaaS site

Real Estate & Property

Buyers and renters browse listings emotionally, at odd hours, on their phones. The site needs to make properties easy to search and beautiful to look at, then turn interest into a scheduled showing before they move on to the next tab.

  • Searchable, filterable listings
  • Large, fast-loading property photography
  • Schedule-a-showing request built into every listing
  • Agent or team bios that build local trust
Discuss a real estate site
