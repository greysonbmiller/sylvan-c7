From the Lab

Square Word Calligraphy

In 1994 the artist Xu Bing invented a way of writing English so that each word becomes one square block that looks like a Chinese character — yet anyone who reads English can read it once they've seen the alphabet. Type below and your words come out the same way.

This tool builds each block from David Kelley's 2012 version of the alphabet, which swaps each letter for a Chinese component that resembles it. Read every block left → right, top → bottom, outside → inside. It was built in-house by Sylvan C7, and it runs entirely in your browser: nothing you type is sent anywhere.

The Rules

How to read a square word

Every letter of the word is there — it's just packed into a square instead of a line. Start at the outside or the top-left and work your way in.

Credits

Whose idea this is

We wrote the software. The idea, the alphabet, and the type all belong to other people.

  • Xu Bing Invented Square Word Calligraphy (英文方块字) in 1994 and taught it through his Square Word Calligraphy Classroom. Read his own account.
  • David B. Kelley Revised the alphabet in 2012, replacing eleven letters with more Chinese-looking forms. His version, documented on Omniglot, is the one this tool draws.
  • Fonts: LXGW WenKai and Noto Serif SC The brush style uses LXGW WenKai and the print styles use Noto Serif SC, both cut down to the few characters the alphabet needs and used under the SIL Open Font License: LXGW WenKai licence, Noto Serif SC licence.

This is an experiment of our own, made because we like the idea. It is not client work, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Xu Bing or David Kelley. Back to how we work.
