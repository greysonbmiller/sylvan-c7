From the Lab

Nuestra Finca

A small hillside farm — a kitchen, a wooden house raised on posts, and the covered bridge between them — rebuilt in 3D from 17 phone photos and a handful of tape-measure numbers. Walk it like a game, or fly over the roofs.

Try It

Walk it yourself

Start the walkthrough, then click inside it (or tap, on a phone) to look around. With a keyboard, Esc opens the menu at any time.

Prefer more room? Open it on its own page, or use the full-screen button in the walkthrough’s menu.

Controls

Getting around

The same list is always one key away inside the walkthrough: press H. On a phone or tablet, drag to look and use the on-screen buttons.

  • W A S D Walk
  • Mouse Look around
  • Shift Run (or fly faster)
  • Space Jump (or rise, when flying)
  • F Switch between walking and flying
  • C Sink down, when flying
  • 1 – 8 Jump to a place: kitchen, bridge, house, patio…
  • L Lamps on or off
  • H Show or hide the controls
  • Esc Menu: places, settings, full screen
The Build

How we made it

From photos to a plan

There were no drawings, just 17 photos and a handful of measurements. We worked out where each photo was taken from, then adjusted the model until it lined up with the pictures: most corners, posts and window edges sit within about 30 cm of where the photos put them.

Built from one table of numbers

The whole site, from posts and braces to roofs, stairs and furniture, is generated by a script from a single list of dimensions. When we learned the house room is actually 4 by 4 metres, the fix was one number and a one-minute rebuild.

Light worked out in advance

Sunlight, the ceiling lamps and the soft shadows are calculated once in Blender and painted onto the surfaces. Your browser just draws them, which is why it runs at around 50–60 frames per second even on basic laptop graphics.

Made to be played

Walls, railings and roofs stop you, the stairs and bridge can be climbed, and the picture sharpens or softens itself to keep the motion smooth on whatever device you’re using.

Credits

What it’s built on

  • Blender The model is built and its lighting calculated in Blender, driven entirely by script.
  • three.js and three-mesh-bvh The walkthrough draws the model with three.js and handles collisions with three-mesh-bvh, both under the MIT licence.

This is an experiment of our own, not client work. The model is approximate: sizes come from photos and a few measurements, and the furniture is placed by eye. Back to the lab.
